What Counts as a Dental Emergency

A dental emergency is any mouth problem that needs fast care to stop pain, stop bleeding, or save a tooth. That includes a tooth that is cracked, broken, or knocked loose, a toothache that will not quit, swelling in the face or gum, bleeding that will not stop, and a crown or filling that came off and left a sharp edge. A small chip with no pain can often wait a day or two, but a loose tooth, a tooth that was knocked out, or pain that keeps you up at night should not wait. If you are not sure, call and ask. An Emergency dentist Palm Beach Gardens team member can hear your story and tell you if you need to come in today or if home care will do until morning.

Frequently Asked Questions

My tooth was knocked out. What do I do first?

Hold it by the top, not the root, rinse it gently without scrubbing, and keep it moist in milk or in your cheek. Call (561) 260-5575 right away and get to the office fast, since a tooth has a better chance of being saved in the first hour.

Can I get a same-day visit for a toothache?

When a tooth hurts a lot, call as early in the day as you can. The team tries to fit urgent tooth pain in fast, and on Sundays they see patients by appointment only. Leave a clear message with your name and number if you call after hours.

What if a crown or filling falls off?

Keep the piece if you can, rinse your mouth gently, and do not chew on that side. A loose crown can sometimes be put back for a short time, but do not glue it yourself. Call the office and bring the piece with you to your visit.

Is swelling in my face a dental emergency?

Yes, swelling in the face or gum can mean an infection that needs fast care. Call the office right away, and tell them about the swelling, any fever, and how bad the pain is. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, go to urgent care or an emergency room right away.

What can I take for tooth pain while I wait?

Use the pain care the team talks about, or the pain care you normally use, and follow the dose on the label. A cold pack on the cheek can help swelling. Do not put a pain pill right on the gum, since it can burn the soft skin there.

Do you see children for emergencies?

Yes, kids are seen for urgent tooth pain, falls, and sports hits. The team uses a slow, calm pace with kids, and they will talk to you and your child about what happened and what comes next.

How much does an emergency visit cost?
